# Works council and employee consultation for HR tech rollouts

Help HR and project teams plan information, consultation and co-determination steps before launching HR systems, AI tools, monitoring or payroll changes. Rules differ widely by country, so verify locally and say so. Search for current national rules when specifics matter.

## Why it matters

Skipping required consultation can delay go-live, invalidate the rollout or trigger sanctions. In many countries, tools that can monitor employee behaviour or performance need the works council's agreement or formal consultation.

## Examples of national approaches (verify before relying on them)

- **Germany:** co-determination rights of the works council over technical systems capable of monitoring employee behaviour or performance. This often applies to HR software, time tracking and analytics, even if monitoring is not the purpose.
- **Netherlands:** works council consent is often needed for systems that process personal data of employees and for monitoring or evaluation systems.
- **France:** the social and economic committee (CSE) must be informed and consulted on new technologies and on changes affecting working conditions.
- **Switzerland:** employees have information and consultation rights, and employers must protect employee personality rights when monitoring.
- **EU level:** the general information and consultation framework and, for transnational companies, European works councils.

## Process to produce

1. **Map countries and bodies:** per country, is there a works council, union, or employee delegation, and what rights apply to this project?
2. **Classify the change:** new system, AI feature, monitoring capability, process change, data transfer to a new provider.
3. **Define what to share:** purpose, data categories, who has access, retention, AI involvement and human oversight, vendor and data location, impact assessment results.
4. **Plan the timeline:** consultation periods can take weeks or months. Work backwards from go-live and mark the earliest start.
5. **Prepare materials:** plain-language summary, data flow diagram, FAQ, and a draft works agreement or agreement framework where relevant.
6. **Track outcomes:** questions raised, commitments made, agreement signed, conditions for go-live.

## Output

Return: a country table (body, right, likely step, lead time), a consultation timeline, a document list, and the top risks. State that this is general information and local labour law counsel should confirm each country.
